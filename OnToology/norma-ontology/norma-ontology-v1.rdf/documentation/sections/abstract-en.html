Abstract

The NORMA ontology (norma-o) is a formal semantic model for representing legal norm annotations as structured, machine-readable knowledge graph resources. It is designed to support the NORMA toolkit, where regulatory requirements are first captured in BPMN-based annotation workflows and then published as semantically explicit artifacts ready for integration, validation, querying, and downstream reasoning.

The ontology organises legal knowledge across four concerns: the type and deontic classification of normative content; the legal context that gives each norm its meaning (conditions, agents, actions, objects, and sources); the provenance and lifecycle metadata of each annotation; and controlled vocabularies for dimensions such as binding force, compliance criticality, extraction method, and review status. Conditional applicability is made explicit through a reified n-ary trigger–event pattern rather than left as unstructured text.

NORMA aligns with established Semantic Web and legal-knowledge standards: PROV-O for provenance, ELI for legal resource modelling, FOAF for agent-oriented metadata, and SKOS for controlled vocabularies. The result is an ontology suited not only for documenting annotations, but also for supporting semantic publication, interoperable exchange, knowledge-graph construction, and explainable compliance applications.
