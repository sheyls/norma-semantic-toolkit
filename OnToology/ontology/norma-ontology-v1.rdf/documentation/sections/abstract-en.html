Abstract

The NORMA ontology provides a formal semantic model for representing machine-readable legal norm annotations as reusable knowledge graph resources. It is designed for settings in which regulatory requirements are analysed, annotated, and operationalised through the NORMA toolkit, especially when legal requirements are first captured in BPMN-based workflows and are subsequently transformed into semantically explicit artifacts suitable for publication, integration, validation, and downstream reasoning.

The ontology models the core structure of annotated legal knowledge at several complementary levels. It distinguishes between regulative norms and constitutive rules; represents legal conditions and their evaluated outcomes; makes conditional activation explicit through a reified trigger-event pattern; and captures the legal agents, actions, objects, sources, and provenance metadata needed for traceability and accountability. In addition, it provides controlled concept schemes for classification tasks such as binding force, compliance criticality, review status, extraction method, and lifecycle status.

NORMA reuses and aligns with established Semantic Web and legal-knowledge standards, notably PROV-O for provenance, ELI for legal resource modelling, FOAF for agent-oriented metadata, and SKOS for controlled vocabularies. The result is an ontology intended not only to document annotations, but also to support professional-grade semantic publication, interoperable exchange, knowledge-graph construction, and explainable compliance-oriented applications.
