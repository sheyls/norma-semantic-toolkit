NORMA ontology: Overview back to ToC

The NORMA ontology is organized around four complementary concerns: (1) normative content and its deontic classification; (2) legal context — the conditions, agents, actions, objects, and sources that give norms their meaning; (3) annotation provenance and lifecycle governance; and (4) controlled concept schemes for classifying key annotation dimensions. The diagram below provides a bird's-eye view of the principal classes and their relationships.

Conceptual groups

Normative content

norma:NormativeContent is the central class. It is specialized into norma:RegulativeNorm — prescriptive statements governing conduct — and norma:ConstitutiveRule — definitional statements that establish legal statuses or categories. Regulative norms are further specialized as norma:Obligation, norma:Prohibition, norma:Permission, norma:Recommendation, and norma:NegativeRecommendation.

Legal context

Each normative content item may be associated with the legal elements that give it meaning: a norma:LegalAgent (the party bearing an obligation or right), a norma:LegalAction (the required, permitted, or prohibited act), a norma:LegalObject (the thing the action concerns), and a norma:LegalSource or norma:LegalSourceExpression (the legal instrument from which the norm derives, aligned with ELI). Literal shortcut properties (norma:agentText, norma:actionText, norma:objectText) support low-overhead annotation pipelines without sacrificing the richer object-level model.

Conditional activation — the TriggerEvent pattern

Legal norms commonly apply only when certain conditions hold. NORMA makes conditional applicability explicit through a reified n-ary pattern: a norma:LegalCondition links to one or more norma:TriggerEvent instances, each pairing a specific norma:ConditionOutcome (true, false, or other) with the norma:NormativeContent that is activated by that outcome. The shortcut property norma:triggersNorm, derived via a property chain, supports systems that need only the existence of a triggering relation.

Provenance and annotation governance

norma:AnnotationActivity (aligned with prov:Activity) records the annotation act that produced a normative content item. norma:AnnotatorAgent (aligned with prov:Agent) identifies the responsible party. Data properties such as norma:confidenceScore, norma:annotationDate, and norma:lastReviewDate support accountability and auditability.

Controlled vocabularies

Six SKOS-based concept classes provide stable, queryable classification values: norma:BindingForce (hard law, soft law, contractual, internal policy), norma:ComplianceCriticality (critical, high, medium, low), norma:ExtractionMethod (manual by lawyer or analyst, LLM extraction, pattern matching, rule-based extraction), norma:NormStatus (active, not yet in force, superseded), norma:ReviewStatus (not reviewed, pending review, under review, approved, disputed), and norma:ConditionOutcome (true outcome, false outcome). Each class has a corresponding SKOS concept scheme named individual (e.g., norma:BindingForceScheme).
