Introduction back to ToC

The NORMA ontology provides a formal model for representing legal norm annotations produced by the NORMA toolkit. It is designed to support settings where legal requirements are captured through BPMN-native annotation practices and subsequently transformed into structured semantic artifacts for use in compliance-oriented workflows.

The ontology models the core elements of a legal norm: what is regulated, by whom, under which conditions, with respect to which actions and objects, and on the basis of which legal sources. It distinguishes between regulative norms, which govern conduct, and constitutive rules, which define or qualify institutional or legal facts. Provenance, review state, lifecycle status, and source traceability are also represented, so that annotations are accountable and reusable across contexts.

Key modelling decisions include: explicit separation of domain entities (legal agents, actions, objects, conditions, and sources); reuse of established vocabularies — PROV-O for provenance, ELI for legal resource modelling, and FOAF for agent descriptions; and an n-ary relation pattern centred on norma:TriggerEvent to represent conditional applicability, capturing not just that a condition exists but which evaluated outcome activates which normative content item.

The ontology supports SPARQL querying, SHACL validation, Linked Data publication, and downstream tasks such as compliance checking and norm conflict detection. It is intended for ontology engineers, legal knowledge engineers, developers building transformation or validation pipelines, and researchers working on the formal representation of legal norms.

This documentation covers namespace declarations, a class and property overview, modelling rationale with examples, a full term-by-term cross-reference, and references to the standards and vocabularies on which NORMA builds.
