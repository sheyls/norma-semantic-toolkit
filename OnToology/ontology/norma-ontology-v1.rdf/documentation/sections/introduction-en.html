Introduction back to ToC

The increasing demand for transparent, interoperable, and machine-processable regulatory knowledge calls for ontology-based representations that preserve legal meaning while remaining operationally useful in compliance-oriented workflows. The NORMA ontology addresses this need by providing the semantic backbone for representing legal norm annotations produced in the NORMA toolkit, particularly in settings where legal requirements are captured through BPMN-native annotation practices and later transformed into structured semantic artifacts. At its core, the ontology is intended to model what is being regulated, under which legal conditions, by whom, with respect to which actions and objects, and on the basis of which legal sources. It therefore distinguishes between regulative norms, which govern conduct, and constitutive rules, which define or qualify institutional or legal facts. In addition, it models provenance, review state, lifecycle status, extraction method, and source traceability so that annotations can be interpreted not only as isolated statements, but as accountable and reusable knowledge objects. The design of the ontology follows several principles commonly found in mature ontology engineering practice. First, domain semantics are made explicit through clearly separated conceptual entities such as legal agents, legal actions, legal objects, legal conditions, and legal sources. Second, the ontology reuses established vocabularies whenever this improves interoperability: PROV-O is used for provenance and attribution, ELI for legal resource modelling, and FOAF for describing agents and project metadata. Third, conditional applicability is represented through an n-ary relation pattern centred on `norma:TriggerEvent`, which makes it possible to state not only that a condition is relevant, but also which evaluated outcome activates which specific normative content item. The intended result is an ontology that is rigorous enough for semantic publication and downstream reasoning, yet practical enough for real annotation pipelines. NORMA is therefore not merely a vocabulary for labelling norms; it is a structured model for connecting legal meaning, source traceability, conditional logic, and annotation provenance in a way that supports inspection, querying, reuse, and future extension. This documentation is written for several audiences: ontology engineers who need a clean conceptual specification; legal knowledge engineers who need a reliable annotation model; developers building transformation, validation, or publication pipelines; and researchers interested in the formal representation of legal norms in machine-readable form. For these audiences, the ontology aims to strike a balance between conceptual clarity, implementation realism, and interoperability with the broader Semantic Web stack. The remainder of this documentation follows a structure familiar from professional ontology specifications. The namespace declarations identify the vocabularies used in the ontology. The overview section summarizes the principal classes, properties, and controlled vocabularies. The description section explains modelling rationale and includes worked examples. The cross-reference section provides the detailed term-by-term specification generated from the ontology itself, while the references section situates NORMA within the standards and design patterns on which it builds.
